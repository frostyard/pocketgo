# 0061 — Reopen a closed thread when a message reaches its specialist

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-22

## Context

**A closed thread's session takes work it cannot report** ([frostyard/pocketgo#55](https://github.com/frostyard/pocketgo/issues/55)). On 2026-11-21 T77 reported `done` on frostyard/core#283 at e0bfba6. Its supervisor closed it and started T84 to take a blocking re-check point. The owner then sent the T77 session a request: take the blocking point and the nits, push, get the re-review, and report. The session pushed 28c0f56, ran `make ci` and updated the pull request. Then `team_message` refused: `thread T77 is closed and takes no more reports`. The supervisor got no report, the re-review's verdict had no thread to go to ([ADR-0019](0019-start-each-cross-review-in-code-with-the-authors-agent-on-the-reviewer-model.md) tells only an author thread that is not closed), and T84 worked on the same branch with nothing to warn either session.

**What closes a thread today, and what follows:**
- `team_close`, the owner's close, a merge of its pull requests ([ADR-0016](0016-link-reported-pull-requests-to-threads-checked-on-github-in-code.md)), a plan's end ([ADR-0020](0020-plan-before-building-with-the-plan-gate-and-consults-in-code.md)), and a start that failed. A close withdraws the thread's queued brief, its worker's replies, its shift's rounds and its supervisor's messages, and drops the ones not placed ([ADR-0032](0032-let-a-supervisor-send-to-a-thread-it-delegated-through-the-outbox.md)). It never touches the owner's messages.
- `team_send` refuses a closed thread (ADR-0032, decision 1). The owner's message to a closed thread's session goes, and nothing follows it.
- Every published close queues the thread's worktree for removal, which the review pass does once the thread's sessions are idle and the tree holds nothing outside its linked pull requests ([ADR-0054](0054-remove-a-closed-threads-worktree-in-code-with-no-github-write.md)). A run in the session after that starts in a folder that is not there.
- A round closes every thread not closed whose pull requests are all merged or closed, one at least merged (ADR-0016).
- A pull request links to one thread, the first that reported it, and the link never moves: another thread's report of it is refused whole (`store.ErrPullTaken`). A successor thread cannot link its predecessor's pull request.

**The owner's decision, 2026-11-22:** the issue's option A, reopen on delivery.
- When the owner's message, or a supervisor's `team_send`, reaches a closed thread's session, the thread goes back to `open`. Delivered means `Engine.Placed` found it, never that `Send` returned, as for the unblocking of [ADR-0037](0037-unblock-a-thread-when-the-supervisors-message-reaches-it.md). `team_message` then works again, and the desktop's board shows the thread reopened.
- When a supervisor closes a thread and starts another for the same pull request, the closed thread names its successor.
- A `team_message` still refused says what the session can do instead: the successor thread, or "ask the owner".
- The issue's open questions (the pull request's link, and where a verdict goes) are to be settled here, with the least surprising answer and [ADR-0019](0019-start-each-cross-review-in-code-with-the-authors-agent-on-the-reviewer-model.md)'s review rules intact.
- The owner dislikes refusals nobody asked for: a rule refuses only what correctness needs.

## Decision

1. **A closed thread goes back to `open` when a message pocketd follows by its key is in its specialist's transcript.** Two messages are followed:
   - **The owner's message** to a thread's specialist session, every one, whatever the thread's state when it is sent. It is followed from when PiG took it, by its request key: the request's completion and the follow are one transaction (`store.CompleteOwnerMessage`, `owner_messages`). A retry of a message an earlier try sent is not followed again, so no noting reopens a thread twice for one message. The message goes, is recorded and is noted under `outboxMu`, which every close holds as it notes the thread's messages: a close comes before the message goes, or after its record, never between (Codex review of #121, round 1). The send reads the thread again under that lock: one that closed since its first look takes the message only if it may reopen, its folder checked too (decision 4; rounds 2 and 3).
   - **A supervisor's `team_send`** kept while the thread was closed (decision 3).

   The noting is the one step that reopens: under `outboxMu`, then `workMu`, in one transaction with the thread's change, and the thread's event goes out with it (`Server.ownerMessagePlaced`, `store.OwnerMessagePlaced`; `store.SendDelivered` for a supervisor's). A placed message reopens a closed thread once; a withdrawn message, or one PiG does not have, stops being followed and reopens nothing; a queued one waits. A message noted while its thread is open changes nothing.
2. **Whatever reads or sets a thread's state notes its placed messages first,** a closed-thread check of the owner's send and of `team_send` included (Codex review of #121, round 4): a report (`team_message`, before its refusal of a closed thread too), `team_worker` on a closed thread, a verdict that tells its author (`team_verdict`, and the review pass's finish of one), a close (the thread's, a closed thread's included, and a plan lead's that the close may end), the owner's stop of a shift, and the delivery pass, every second while one waits in a closed thread's queue. So a message placed before a close is noted while the thread is open and reopens nothing after it, and the report a run files after the owner's message is taken. The owner's send notes its own message at once.
3. **`team_send` takes a closed thread** it may reopen (decision 4). Its text says that it reopens the thread if the thread is still closed when it arrives, and nothing of a blocked thread's asks (`team.SendTextToClosed`; round 3); it is kept with the reopen its delivery brings, the thread's reopen count after it (`reports.reopens`), and no unblocking (`reports.unblocks` false; `store.FileSendTo`). It goes to the closed thread and reopens it once placed. A reopen by another message first makes it a message to an open thread; a close after that reopen withdraws and drops it, as every close drops a supervisor's queued message (ADR-0032, decision 3), and a pass that finds it unplaced on a thread closed since drops it. A close drops a thread's messages that go only while it is open (`team.DroppedAtClose`: a worker's, a shift's, a supervisor's) in the transaction that closes it (`store.threadClosed`), so a stop of pocketd after the close leaves none for a reopen to send, and migration 24 marks those a close before it left unsent (round 3); a close of a thread closed already drops none, so a message that would reopen it still goes (Codex review of #121, round 1). A text written for one state is kept only while the thread is in it, after the thread's placed messages are noted under `outboxMu` held through the keeping; a thread that moved in between is read and checked again, once (Codex review of #121, round 2).
4. **A message that could not reopen its closed thread is refused before it goes,** to the owner with `409 conflict`, to the supervisor as the tool's refusal, each naming what to do instead (decision 7):
   - a plan's own thread, whose close ended its plan, and a plan task's, whose task the plan settled at the close (`team.CheckReopen`);
   - a thread whose brief never reached its specialist;
   - a thread whose folder is no longer its own, as a worktree [ADR-0054](0054-remove-a-closed-threads-worktree-in-code-with-no-github-write.md) removed (`team.FolderGone`, from `Server.recheckFolder`): its specialist could not work there.

   A check that cannot tell refuses nothing and sends nothing (`500 internal`). The owner's message sent while the thread was open, and placed after a plan's close, reopens nothing either: the store reopens no plan's thread, and pocketd stops following a closed plan thread's messages, which go on to its session as PiG has them (round 4).
5. **A closed thread's worktree stays while a message that may reopen it is on its way.** Each closed thread has a cleanup lock (`Server.lockClean`), taken before `outboxMu` and `workMu`: the removal holds it from its look at the thread to git's end, and a message to the closed thread holds it from the look at its folder to the record that follows the message. The removal reads the thread and whether such a message waits from one snapshot (`store.CleanState`), and waits while the thread has an owner's message followed or a supervisor's reopening message not noted; a thread no longer closed is decided at its next close, not now. Each decision is of one close, by the thread's reopen count: a kept worktree's line not yet written for a close a reopen ended is dropped, and lands on no later close (`store.NoteClosedThreadAt`; Codex review of #121, round 1).
6. **What a reopen does:** the thread is `open`, with its last report; its reopen count goes up, `reopened_at` is now, and `reopen_seq` keeps the order of GitHub's reads then (`pull_reads`). A round closes it by its pull requests again only by a merge of one a read that began after the reopen found open (`pull_requests.open_seq`, `team.MergedOut`), so a merge that came before the reopen, though first read after it, never closes it; a merge no read saw open after the reopen leaves it for its supervisor or the owner to close (Codex review of #121, round 4). It takes its place on the desk again, over the cap if need be: the cap bounds new delegations, and each later close frees it again, with a note of its own to a plan the cap held (round 2), only once the desk is below the cap the plan was held at (`plans.held_cap`), a close of the plan's own task included (rounds 4 and 5). Its shift stays ended, and its worker is its own again. A close owes its worker a stop (`threads.worker_stop_due`), and no message reopens the thread until that stop is done: the message stays followed, or unnoted, and reopens the thread once the run of the closed time stopped, after a restart too, so no stop reaches the reopened thread's work; the stop's end moves the worker's cursor past the closed time, so none of its runs replies to the specialist (`store.WorkerStopped`; Codex review of #121, rounds 2 to 6). A merge's close reads the thread again under the team's locks before it withdraws anything (round 3). A merge's close reads the thread again under the team's locks before it withdraws anything (round 3). Its pull requests are published with it, since a client lists a closed thread's only while open, and by a delivery pass when they cannot be read then (rounds 2 and 3). A plan's thread is never reopened.
7. **Every refusal on a closed thread says where to go instead.**
   - **The successor:** `team_delegate` takes `follows`, the thread the new one carries on: one a session of the caller's profile delegated, in any state, no cross-review, and no plan work (`team.CheckFollows`). The followed thread names the new one as its `successor` in the transaction that records it, with its event; the newest follower wins. pocketd names the first successor that is not closed, following at most 16 (`team.MaxSuccessors`); each is newer, so the chain has no loop.
   - **`team_message`'s refusal on a closed thread** (`team.ReportRefused`): its work goes on in the successor, so the specialist ends its turn with what it did in its reply and asks the owner to pass it on; or, for a plan's thread, the plan settled it; or the specialist asks the owner, since a message from the owner or the supervisor reopens the thread.
   - **A refused message** (decision 4) names the successor, or tells the owner to ask the supervisor to delegate the work again, and the supervisor to delegate it with `follows`.
   - **A report that names a pull request another thread links** is refused before GitHub is read, under the pull request's lock, and says to report again without it (rounds 1 and 4).
   - **The owner's close run again** with its request id answers with the thread as it is, its placed messages noted first, and closes nothing again: a message may have reopened it since (Codex review of #121, rounds 1 and 5).
8. **A pull request's link does not move.** The reopened thread does not take it back, and two threads never link it: it stays with the thread that first reported it (ADR-0016), whatever closes or reopens. In the T77 case, T77 reopens still linking core#283, and its next report and the reviews' verdicts reach it. A successor that reports its predecessor's pull request is refused, as now, and told to report without it. A link that moves with each report would let two live threads take a pull request from each other, each run moving its reviews' verdicts and its label's backing, and nothing in GitHub's answer says which thread's push a head is.
9. **A verdict goes to the thread that links its pull request,** when that thread is not closed (ADR-0019, unchanged). pocketd cannot tell which session pushed a head: pushes come from the sandbox, and GitHub's answer names no session. A rule by "the thread that last pushed" would rest on a model's word, against [ADR-0011](0011-put-rules-in-code-not-in-model-judgment.md). With decision 1, the thread a message reopened gets the verdicts of the reviews that finish after its reopen.
10. **The desktop shows it, by rules in `desktop/internal/state`:** a card marked `reopened` while a reopened thread is not closed again (`state.Reopened`), and a line naming the thread it carries on and the one that carries it on (`state.ThreadLinks`). The board groups a reopened thread by `Data.NextMover`, as any. A close's reply never undoes a copy with more reopens, which is of a later lifetime (`Data.ThreadClosed`; Codex review of #121, round 1), and one that is not closed, a close run again after a reopen, is a snapshot that applies only over an older copy (round 2): copies are ordered by lifetime, then by closure within it, then by `updatedAt`, for a close's reply, an event after it and a fetch's copy alike, in both directions (`lifetimeNewer`; rounds 3 to 6), and a close whose thread reopened since it was asked for retires its request id (`Store.settleCloses`; round 3).
11. **Protocol version 32** adds a thread's `follows`, `successor`, `reopens` and `reopenedAt`, a `thread` event when a thread reopens, the conflict of the owner's message to a closed thread that cannot reopen, `team_send` to a closed thread, and `team_delegate`'s `follows` (rules 1, 10, 24, 26, 27, 28a, 30a and 39). **Work database migration 24** adds `threads.follows`, `successor`, `reopened`, `reopened_at` and `reopen_seq`, `pull_requests.open_seq`, `plans.held_cap`, `threads.worker_stop_due`, `reports.reopens` and the `owner_messages` table.

## Consequences

- A request to a closed thread ends with a report: the owner's message or the supervisor's reopens the thread, and the specialist's report goes to the supervisor, its pull requests are read and reviewed again, and the board shows it with its specialist.
- A supervisor that carries a thread on in another names it, and a late message to the old one, or its specialist's late report, is pointed at the new one.
- The owner's messages to specialists are written to the work database and looked up until placed; one queued for a busy specialist of an open thread holds no delivery pass back.
- A closed thread whose worktree pocketd removed takes no message. ADR-0054's consequence, that a run in that session starts in a folder that is not there, no longer comes from a message: the message is refused with where to go.
- Protocol version 32 and migration 24: the desktop must be rebuilt, and a restored work database at 23 is upgraded at the next start.
- **Known limits:**
  - A message whose record a stop of pocketd lost, between PiG taking it and its record, is not followed, and reopens nothing; its specialist's report is refused, with the pointer.
  - A verdict filed while its author's thread was closed reaches no one, as before: the reopened thread learns of it from the label, or the next review.
  - Nothing tells a successor that its predecessor reopened: the supervisor learns it from the reopened thread's next report, and the board shows both threads with their link. Two sessions may still work on one branch.
  - A message an idle session holds in its queue after a failed run waits for the session's next input (protocol rule 29); until then the closed thread's worktree stays.
  - A successor cannot link its predecessor's pull request (decision 8); a home successor's work on it is in its reports' text.

## Alternatives considered

- **Refuse up front (the issue's option B):** the owner chose to reopen.
- **Reopen when `Send` returns:** a message an abort withdrew would reopen a thread that got no work, and a queued one would reopen it before its specialist saw it.
- **Follow only the owner's messages sent while the thread is closed:** a message queued over a close would run, and its report be refused, the issue's failure with another timing.
- **Recreate a removed worktree on reopen:** a new branch and fetch rule for a rare case; the refusal names the thread that carries the work on.
- **Let the link move to the thread that last reported the pull request, or let both threads link it:** see decision 8; a pull request's verdicts and label would follow whichever run reported last.
- **Name the successor from the pull request a new thread reports:** a successor cannot link its predecessor's pull request, and the brief's text is the model's; `follows` is the supervisor's explicit act, checked in code.
- **Tell the successor's session when its predecessor reopens:** another outbox message to a session at work; the supervisor coordinates the two, and gets the reopened thread's report.
